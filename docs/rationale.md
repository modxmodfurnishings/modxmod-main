# Rationale

Furniture and furnishings accessories are often permanent in our lives. We buy them once and treasure them. We covet "heirloom quality". But what happens when the circumstances of our lives change, or when we want to gift an item to someone else?

What often happens is these objects get thrown away into landfills. How might we prevent this waste? How do we enable repairability? How do we empower people to reconfigure the products they have? When we gift the products, how do the recipients suit their own taste?

This is why all MODxMOD FURNISHINGS products were designed with modularity in mind. Here's how we have attempted to achieve this:

- Create consistent naming of parts and attachment methods
- Use consistent sizes and placement of holes
- Use commonplace materials and widely available fasteners
- Provide open specifications and documentation
- Encourage people to modify and hack the products to their needs
- Separate the internal support structure from the outward facing veneers
- Create a community of others interested in building upon these modular systems
- Recognize that no product lasts forever and must be repaired

## Architecture vs. style

A house or an office building is a good metaphor for the Open Furnishings System (OFS):

- The structural members and room shapes are rarely changed
- The decorations and furniture in them continually change

Why can't we design furniture this way?

To enable multiple uses of the same piece of furniture, there needs to be a notion of which parts are the unchanging structure of the object versus the always-changing veneer.

In OFS, this is expressed with solid bases and rigid boxes that are fastened together to create larger structures. The dimensions of these architectural objects should be as consistent as possible.

## Creating the grid

We favor the metric system using multiples of 32mm:

- Most accessory trays are 256x128mm
- Most box units have face dimensions of 384x384mm and depths of 384mm or 512mm
- Holes that connect larger units are bored on a 128mm grid
- Holes that connect minor attachments are bored on a 32mm grid

Shelving pins and door hardware adhere to the 32mm system:

- Door hinges and cups
- Shelf pin placement
- Handles and pulls

However in North America many rods and knobs use Imperial measurements:

- 3/4", 1", and 1-1/2" wooden dowels
- 1" grommet caps

## Why CC0 and open source

The world is full of products that don't interoperate. To achieve widespread compatibility we have to focus on what matters: naming, sizes, and placement. What we don't need are legal and IP hurdles blocking innovation. This repository is a toolkit help spur your imagination and creativity.

By creating a Github repository we can create folders to group similar items together. We then store guides, templates, designs, and models in the folders. We can continually update this repository and you can subscribe to notifications. You can also propose contributions.

Creative Commons Zero is a way for us to ensure that no one can own the basic intellectual property of the system: the system isn't the important part, it's the products and the craftsmanship of each object. Let's focus on bringing more art, more usability, and more flexibility into our homes. 

CC0 does not prevent you from creating your own IP, patents, and designs that are derivative of this work. You should certainly be able to protect your unique products. However, you should not be able to claim the basic framework since all of us need to rely on OFS as a kind of shared utility.

## The future of OFS / how you can help

OFS is just beginning and has a lot of room to grow. We envision that there will be several bodies that govern:

- Naming, sizes, and interoperability specifications
- Prototyping and user testing
- Education and outreach, including documentation and training
- Managing a growing community with moderation and creating safe spaces for people to openly contribute and respectfully debate solutions
- Creating in-person events to let us celebrate our collaborative solutions and also show other people why OFS can benefit them
